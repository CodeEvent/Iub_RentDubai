Governing law

Every notice cites the statute it relies on

  • UAE Federal Law No. (33) of 2008, Amending Law No. (26) of 2007 Regulating the Relationship Between Landlords and Tenants in the Emirate of Dubai.
  • Article 25(2): a 12-month (365-day) notice for sale, personal use/recovery, demolition, or extensive renovation — served before the current contract's expiry.
  • Article 25(1): a 30-day notice for non-payment of rent or unauthorized subleasing.
  • Article 25(3): notice must be served by Notary Public, registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt, or court bailiff — not email, SMS, or verbal notice.
  • Regulated by Dubai's Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) and enforceable before the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC).

FAQ

Common questions about eviction notices in Dubai

Is RentShield RERA compliant?

Yes. Every notice cites UAE Federal Law No. (33) of 2008 (Amending Law No. (26) of 2007 Regulating the Relationship Between Landlords and Tenants in the Emirate of Dubai) and the specific Article 25 provision it relies on — the same statute RERA and the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC) enforce.

How long is the eviction notice period in Dubai?

It depends on the reason. Selling the property, personal use/recovery, demolition, or extensive renovation require a 12-month (365-day) notice under Article 25(2), served before the current contract's expiry. Non-payment of rent or unauthorized subleasing only require 30 days under Article 25(1). RentShield picks the correct period automatically from the reason you select.

Does an eviction notice need to be notarized?

Article 25(3) requires the notice to be served through one of three specific channels: a Notary Public, registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt, or a court bailiff — a WhatsApp message, plain email, or verbal notice doesn't satisfy the law, however clearly it communicates intent. RentShield's optional Certified E-Signature add-on (AED {{ add_ons.certified_esignature.price_aed }}) routes your notice through a certified signing and delivery workflow after you generate it. The Real Notary Public add-on (AED {{ add_ons.real_notarization.price_aed }}) goes further — an actual licensed UAE notary, not just certified e-signature — fulfilled by a real notary-services contact rather than an instant API, so it takes longer than the other add-ons but produces a genuinely notarized copy.

Do I need to go to court or file with Dubai Land Department to serve a notice?

No — serving the notice itself doesn't require a court filing. If the tenant doesn't comply once the notice period ends, the next step is filing a case at Dubai's Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC), not the courts directly. RentShield's Legal Skills library (available once you're signed in) covers the specific RDSC filing requirements.

Can I generate and serve an eviction notice online?

You can generate it online in minutes — a real bilingual PDF with the correct statutory citations and notice period, stored as a searchable document. Serving it still has to go through one of the three channels Article 25(3) recognizes (see above), which is exactly why the Certified E-Signature add-on exists — to route an online-generated notice through a certified delivery workflow rather than leaving you to arrange that separately.

Do you provide an eviction notice template?

Not a static template you fill in yourself — RentShield generates the actual bilingual (English/Arabic) notice from your specific details, picks the correct statutory or breach notice period automatically, and cites the exact Article 25 provision it relies on. It's stored as a real, searchable document, not a one-off file you have to track.

What is the process to serve an eviction notice in Dubai?

Three steps, matching how RentShield generates it: pick the reason (which determines whether it's a 12-month statutory notice under Article 25(2) or a 30-day breach notice under Article 25(1)); generate the bilingual, statute-cited notice; then serve it through one of the three channels Article 25(3) recognizes — Notary Public, registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt, or court bailiff.

What are the valid reasons for a landlord to evict a tenant in Dubai?

Under Article 25(2), four reasons qualify for the 12-month notice: selling the property, personal use/recovery, demolition, or extensive renovation. Under Article 25(1), two reasons qualify for the 30-day notice: non-payment of rent or unauthorized subleasing. RentShield's notice form only offers these six statutory reasons — nothing else.

Can a tenant dispute or challenge an eviction notice in Dubai?

Yes — a tenant can challenge a notice at Dubai's Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC), the body that enforces Article 25. RentShield's Legal Skills library (available once you're signed in) covers what's typically needed for an RDSC filing, from either side.

What happens if a landlord doesn't follow the correct eviction procedure?

A notice served the wrong way — the wrong period for the reason given, or through a method Article 25(3) doesn't recognize — risks being unenforceable if it's disputed at the RDSC. That's the specific gap RentShield closes: the period and service-method guidance are tied directly to the reason and the statute, not left for you to work out.

Is there a 90-day eviction notice in Dubai?

Not under Law No. (33) of 2008's Article 25, the statute RentShield implements — the two periods that exist there are 30 days (Article 25(1)) and 12 months /365 days (Article 25(2)); there's no separate 90-day eviction category. If you've seen "90 days" mentioned elsewhere, double-check whether it's actually describing a different process (e.g. a rent-increase notice) rather than an eviction notice — RentShield generates eviction notices only, and none of this is legal advice.

What's the difference between "Certified E-Signature" and registered mail?

Article 25(3) treats them as two separate, independently valid channels — you only need one. RentShield's Certified E-Signature add-on routes your notice through a certified e-signature and delivery workflow; registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt is the other route Article 25(3) names, typically sent through Emirates Post's registered mail/registered digital communication service (TRA-accredited, produces a legally admissible delivery certificate). RentShield doesn't currently dispatch registered mail directly on your behalf — if you'd rather go that route, generate your notice here and send it through Emirates Post yourself.
