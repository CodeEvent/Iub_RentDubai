How it works

Three steps from reason to a served notice

No template shopping, no guessing which notice period applies. RentShield knows the difference between a 12-month statutory ground and a 30-day breach ground, and builds the right notice either way.

1
Select your reason

Sale, personal use, demolition, or renovation trigger the 365-day statutory notice under Article 25(2); non-payment or unauthorized subleasing trigger the 30-day breach notice under Article 25(1). RentShield applies the correct period automatically.

2
Generate the bilingual notice

A real PDF is rendered in English and Arabic with the applicable clause and citation, then stored as a document in your own paperless-ngx instance — full-text searchable, tagged, and versioned like everything else you keep there.

3
Notarize & review, optionally

Route the notice through a licensed notary for physical notarization, or run an AI compliance review against an uploaded tenancy contract to flag clauses that fall short of Article 25 before you rely on them.

Who it's for

Access scoped to who someone actually is

RentShield's roles aren't a UI label — they're enforced on every document, by paperless-ngx's own permission system. A Property Owner sees the notices they generated; a Notary only sees notices once notarization is actually requested; a Lawyer only sees sensitive-reason notices; nobody sees more than their role allows by default.

Governing law

Every notice cites the statute it relies on

  • UAE Federal Law No. (33) of 2008, Amending Law No. (26) of 2007 Regulating the Relationship Between Landlords and Tenants in the Emirate of Dubai.
  • Article 25(2): a 12-month (365-day) notice for sale, personal use/recovery, demolition, or extensive renovation — served before the current contract's expiry.
  • Article 25(1): a 30-day notice for non-payment of rent or unauthorized subleasing.
  • Article 25(3): notice must be served by Notary Public, registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt, or court bailiff — not email, SMS, or verbal notice.
  • Regulated by Dubai's Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) and enforceable before the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC).
